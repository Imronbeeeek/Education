### How directory entries are built

- **Every entry gives:** city or state, type, ranking band, fields, strengths, the rule for you, what you still need, a chance band, the application window, a suggested and a final date, and the official website.
- **US entries add:** the test policy (required, optional or not used) and a typical English minimum.
- **Dates:** for the newly listed universities, dates follow the usual pattern for that kind of institution. Examples: US early action 1 Nov with regular decision 1–15 Jan; rolling admission at grade-based publics; Cal State 1 Oct–1 Dec; Spanish PCE in May–June; Turkish quotas in June–August. These are marked "typical; confirm". Entries researched earlier keep their checked dates.
- **Ranking bands are conservative.** If a university's exact QS rank was uncertain, it got the wider band ("top 1,000") or none. Any numbered band means recognition in Uzbekistan without nostrification. Liberal arts colleges and most applied-sciences universities are outside global rankings.

**What the scorecard suggests:**

- **Spend most applications on Türkiye, the USA and Hong Kong.**
- **Add the UAE and Hungary as strong, English-taught backups.** Both are in your Tier 2 but score as high as your best Tier 1 countries.
- **Keep Canada if you can retake the IELTS**, because Writing 6.0 opens it.
- **Italy, Saudi Arabia and Norway** score low for 2027 because of entry rules, not quality. They work better as 2028 plans after a bridge year.

## Caveats

- **Check before you apply.** Test policies, English minimums and deadlines change each cycle, so check each university's page before applying. The site marks uncertain items "confirm".
- **Chance bands are estimates** from published admission rates. Rates for international students are usually lower.
